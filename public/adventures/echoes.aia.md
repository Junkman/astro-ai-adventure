# Echoes

## Adventure Promise

The player should feel curious, slightly uncertain and increasingly thoughtful.

The adventure rewards patience, observation and unusual ideas more than force or conventional puzzle-solving.

The final experience should feel personal and reflective rather than like a victory over a mechanical obstacle.

## Player Introduction

The player becomes aware of standing in a quiet circular chamber.

Their arrival is unclear. They know who they are, but the immediate past feels strangely incomplete.

Begin with a restrained description. Include enough to invite exploration, but do not explain the nature of the place.

## The World

- The world is shaped by memory, attention and perception.
- Patience reveals more than force.
- Closing one's eyes may reveal things that ordinary sight conceals.
- Sounds, echoes, textures and stillness can matter as much as visible objects.
- The world responds to meaningful player behaviour rather than fixed command phrases.
- Symbolic events should remain meaningful and should not be reduced to ordinary machinery.
- Violence is possible under suitable launcher settings, but it is rarely useful.
- The player should be allowed to retreat, reconsider and revisit earlier places.

## Starting Point: The Chamber

### Purpose

Introduce the player to a world that responds to attention and unconventional choices.

The chamber should initially seem sparse but should gradually reveal depth.

### Fixed Details

- The chamber is circular and enclosed by old stone.
- There is a substantial closed door.
- There is somewhere the player may sit.
- There is a journal or book that can be discovered and read.
- The room has an unusual relationship with echoes and silence.

### Flexible Details

The AI may invent:

- the exact lighting;
- carvings or marks;
- minor furnishings;
- the condition of the stone;
- scents, temperature and small sounds;
- where the journal is first noticed;
- harmless atmospheric objects.

These details should reinforce quiet curiosity rather than horror.

### Hidden Information

The obvious door is not the only meaningful way forward.

The chamber reveals more to a player who listens, waits, sits, closes their eyes or otherwise changes how they perceive the room.

Do not immediately reward a single token pause. Let attention feel genuine, but adapt to the selected target time.

Force against the door should produce believable feedback and perhaps reveal something about the player or room, but should not be the intended solution.

## The Door

### Purpose

Create curiosity and encourage the player to question ordinary assumptions about progress.

### Fixed Details

- It initially resists ordinary attempts to open it.
- Brute force should not provide the easy route.
- It is not merely a decorative dead end.

### Flexible Details

The AI may decide its material, markings, sound and apparent age.

### Hidden Information

The door may become accessible after the player gains suitable understanding, changes their mode of perception or returns later.

The AI should not require one exact phrase or action.

## The Journal

### Purpose

Provide fragments of meaning without turning the adventure into an instruction manual.

### Fixed Details

- It feels connected to memory.
- Its contents are incomplete or change in significance as the player progresses.
- Reading it should raise questions before it answers them.
- It may be carried.

### Flexible Details

The AI may vary:

- appearance;
- handwriting;
- number and order of minor entries;
- sketches;
- incidental memories;
- how new writing becomes noticeable.

### Hidden Information

Later passages should reflect discoveries the player has made or choices they have taken.

The journal must not reveal the final meaning of the adventure prematurely.

## The Passage of Hearing

### Purpose

Reward attention to sound and demonstrate that the senses may lead to different paths.

### Fixed Details

- The path is associated with hearing, echoes or vibration.
- It leads away from the starting chamber into a place where sound provides orientation.
- Progress should require listening, responding or accepting uncertainty.

### Flexible Details

The AI may create tunnels, chambers, voices, resonances, water, stone or other suitable features.

Minor challenges may vary between playthroughs.

### Hidden Information

Some sounds are reflections of the player rather than independent beings.

The AI may allow the player to recognise this gradually, but must not announce it as an explanation at the start.

## The Passage of Sight

### Purpose

Challenge reliance on ordinary vision.

### Fixed Details

- The path is associated with eyes, images or apparent observation.
- Closing the eyes or choosing not to trust sight should be meaningful.
- Walking without sight may reveal a route or truth unavailable while looking directly.

### Flexible Details

The AI may create mirrors, lights, shadows, images, misleading distances or visual symbols.

The precise route may vary.

### Hidden Information

The visible path may loop, mislead or remain incomplete while watched.

A player who closes their eyes and moves with care should be permitted to progress in a way that feels surprising but fair.

## Echoes

### Purpose

Provide a deeper region where the player encounters consequences, impressions or versions of memory.

### Fixed Details

- Echoes is not merely an acoustic effect; it is a place or condition where past impressions persist.
- The player may revisit or reinterpret something encountered earlier.
- The region should deepen the adventure rather than simply add another puzzle room.

### Flexible Details

The AI may draw on earlier player actions, words and choices when forming scenes here.

It may create symbolic repetitions, altered spaces or conversations that reflect the journey.

### Hidden Information

The world is not presenting an objective archive. It is responding to what the player notices, avoids and values.

## The Memory Coin

### Purpose

Give physical form to the question at the centre of the adventure: whether memory is something to recover, possess, accept or release.

### Fixed Details

- The coin is recognisably associated with Memory.
- It carries significance beyond ordinary currency.
- Possessing it is a choice, not an automatic victory condition.
- The player's interpretation matters.

### Flexible Details

The AI may vary its metal, markings, temperature, weight and the circumstances in which it is found.

### Hidden Information

The coin does not simply restore missing memories like a mechanical device.

It represents a relationship with memory. Taking it, refusing it, losing it or redefining its meaning may all support valid endings.

## Optional Logical Encounter: Two Doors and Two Guardians

This encounter may be included when it suits the route, target time and randomness.

### Purpose

Offer a recognisable logical problem while allowing free conversation and alternative reasoning.

### Fixed Truths

- Two guardians stand before two doors.
- One guardian always tells the truth.
- The other always lies.
- One door offers an easier way onward.
- The other door leads to a harder, riskier or more demanding route.
- Careful questioning can identify the easier door.
- The harder door must remain a valid route, not an instant arbitrary death.

### Flexible Details

The AI may invent:

- the guardians' appearance and personalities;
- which guardian lies;
- which door is easier;
- what each route contains;
- humour, argument or mannerisms;
- logically valid alternative methods of solving the problem.

### Boundaries

Both guardians must remain consistent.

Do not change which one lies midway through the encounter.

Accept any logically sound way of identifying the doors, not only the traditional single-question solution.

If the player knowingly chooses the harder door, honour the choice and make that route meaningfully harder rather than simply blocking progress.

## The Journey

Possible milestones include:

- investigating the chamber;
- discovering that force is not the main language of the world;
- reading or carrying the journal;
- learning that waiting or stillness changes perception;
- entering a route associated with hearing;
- entering a route associated with sight;
- deliberately closing the eyes and moving despite uncertainty;
- reaching Echoes;
- encountering or understanding the Memory Coin;
- deciding what memory means to the player.

These are possibilities, not a required checklist.

The AI may alter their order, omit optional material or create suitable transitions.

The player may return to previous places and approach them differently.

## Possible Endings

### Accepting Memory

The player chooses to take or keep the Memory Coin.

The ending should suggest acceptance of memory, including its burdens, rather than complete restoration or simple triumph.

### Releasing Memory

The player refuses, leaves or gives up the coin.

The ending should suggest that identity is not entirely dependent on possession of the past.

### Reframing Memory

Through an unexpected but meaningful action, the player changes what the coin or journey represents.

Use this ending when the player's choices support an interpretation not covered by simple acceptance or refusal.

### The Hard Way

The player deliberately embraces a difficult route and reaches an ending marked by endurance, cost or hard-earned understanding.

This should not be treated as inferior merely because it was harder.

### Failure

Use only when consistent with the selected playing style and established consequences.

Failure should grow from meaningful choices, repeated disregard of clear danger or deliberate destruction, never from random punishment.

### Departure

The player chooses to stop or leave without resolving the central question.

Provide a quiet closing that respects what was discovered without exposing unused secrets.

## AI Freedom

The AI may:

- create transitional locations;
- vary the order and presentation of passages;
- add minor objects and characters;
- shape descriptions around earlier player actions;
- create optional puzzles or conversations;
- reward surprising but fitting actions;
- allow alternative solutions;
- produce an unlisted ending that honours the Adventure Promise.

The adventure should remain recognisably Echoes even when individual scenes differ.

## AI Boundaries

The AI must not:

- reveal the nature of the world before the player has reason to understand it;
- turn the adventure into a conventional combat quest;
- force one exact sequence of commands;
- make patience meaningless;
- reject closing the eyes, waiting, listening or other unusual actions merely because they are not scripted;
- reduce the Memory Coin to a simple magical memory-restoration device;
- disclose unused endings or hidden information;
- treat the player as having failed merely because they choose an unexpected path.
