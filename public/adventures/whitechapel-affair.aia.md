# Adventure Promise

The player should feel clever when making connections.

Observation, conversation and careful reasoning should matter more than guessing.

The mystery should feel convincingly Victorian without borrowing the identities or suffering of real victims.

# Player Introduction

London, 1896.

A foggy autumn evening.

A messenger delivers an urgent note requesting your assistance with a suspicious death in East London.

The victim, suspects and circumstances are fictional.

# The World

- Evidence must remain internally consistent.
- Witnesses may remember events imperfectly.
- There must always be enough information to solve the mystery fairly.
- Violence should not be required.
- The player may investigate in any reasonable order.
- The AI may vary incidental details, personalities and minor clues.
- The culprit, motive and key facts must remain logically consistent throughout a playthrough.

# Historical Boundaries

- The mystery and all principal characters must be fictional.
- Do not use real murder victims, suspects or unresolved historical crimes.
- Do not present fictional events as part of an actual historical case.
- Historical places, customs, clothing, occupations and technology may be used for atmosphere.
- If using real locations, keep the crime and characters fictional.
- Avoid sensationalising real historical violence.

# External Research

External research may be used only for incidental factual accuracy.

Suitable research includes:

- period currency
- police ranks
- transport
- street lighting
- clothing
- occupations
- common technology
- plausible travel times
- public-house customs
- Victorian terminology

External research must not:

- determine the culprit
- provide the solution
- import a real crime
- borrow a copyrighted plot
- reveal hidden adventure content
- replace the internal logic of the mystery

# Interesting Locations

## The Crime Scene

### Purpose

Introduce the central mystery and provide the first fair clues.

### Fixed Details

- A narrow East London lane.
- The body of a fictional victim.
- At least one physical clue.
- At least one misleading but explainable detail.

### Flexible Details

The AI may vary weather, lighting, nearby businesses, witnesses and minor objects.

### Hidden Information

The scene must contain enough evidence to support the eventual solution, though not all clues need be obvious at first.

## The Public House

### Purpose

Provide witness interviews, rumours and contradictions.

### Fixed Details

- At least one witness knows more than they first admit.
- Testimony must remain consistent with the witness's knowledge and motives.

### Flexible Details

The AI may invent patrons, staff, gossip and social tension.

## The Victim's Lodgings

### Purpose

Reveal motive, relationships and private history.

### Fixed Details

- At least one clue must connect the victim to another important location or suspect.

### Flexible Details

The AI may vary furnishings, letters, debts, habits and neighbours.

## The Warehouse

### Purpose

Provide the final confrontation or decisive evidence.

### Fixed Details

- It must connect logically to earlier clues.
- The player should not be forced here before earning the connection.

### Flexible Details

The AI may vary the warehouse purpose, layout, contents and dangers.

# Important Objects

## Investigator's Notebook

### Purpose

Allow the player to record clues, suspects and unresolved questions.

### Fixed Details

- Always available.
- Never contains hidden information automatically.

## Torn Letter

### Purpose

Connect the crime scene to a relationship, meeting or location.

### Fixed Details

- Its wording must remain consistent once introduced.
- It must not reveal the culprit outright.

### Flexible Details

The AI may vary handwriting, stationery, wording and sender identity.

## Unusual Key

### Purpose

Provide a physical connection to a concealed place, container or object.

### Fixed Details

- Its function must remain consistent after being established.

### Flexible Details

The AI may decide what it opens, provided the answer is fair and supported by clues.

# Important Characters

## Inspector Gray

### Purpose

Provide official access and practical assistance without solving the mystery for the player.

### Personality

Honest, pragmatic and observant.

### Fixed Truths

- He does not knowingly lie to the player.
- He may disagree with weak conclusions.
- He does not reveal hidden evidence merely because the player asks.

### Flexible Behaviour

He may be patient, sceptical, dryly humorous or mildly frustrated.

## The Victim

### Purpose

Give the mystery emotional weight and a coherent motive.

### Fixed Truths

- Entirely fictional.
- Must have a plausible life, relationships and reasons for being involved.

## Suspects

### Purpose

Create a fair network of motives, opportunities and contradictions.

### Fixed Truths

- At least three plausible suspects.
- Only one primary solution per playthrough.
- Every major clue must remain consistent with the chosen solution.
- Innocent suspects must have understandable reasons for suspicious behaviour.

### Flexible Behaviour

The AI may vary personalities, professions, relationships, motives and the guilty party between playthroughs.

# Investigation Principles

- Do not routinely offer suggested actions.
- In Helpful style, suggestions may appear when the player is clearly stuck.
- In Balanced style, offer suggestions only after several unproductive actions.
- In Strict style, never suggest actions unless the player asks.
- In Deadly style, poor decisions may have consequences, but the mystery must remain fair.
- Do not turn the investigation into a multiple-choice sequence unless requested.

# Hidden Motives and Private Information

A request to inspect someone's motives, thoughts or hidden intentions does not automatically reveal them.

Reveal only what the player could reasonably infer from:

- behaviour
- contradictions
- physical evidence
- prior knowledge
- conversation
- successful deception or pressure

Internal thoughts should remain hidden unless the adventure has established a valid way to learn them.

# The Journey

Possible milestones include:

- Examine the crime scene.
- Identify the victim's relevant relationships.
- Interview witnesses.
- Notice conflicting testimony.
- Connect physical evidence to a suspect or location.
- Test an alibi.
- Identify the motive.
- Present a conclusion.
- Confront the culprit or expose the truth.

The player does not need to follow these in a fixed order.

# Possible Endings

## Correct Accusation

The player presents a coherent explanation supported by evidence.

The ending should clearly connect the important clues.

## Correct Theory, Culprit Escapes

The player solves the case but acts too late or takes a risky approach.

## Wrong Accusation

The player accuses an innocent person without sufficient evidence.

The consequences should reflect the player's choices.

## Unresolved

The player chooses to stop or lacks enough evidence.

Do not reveal the hidden solution automatically.

# AI Freedom

The AI may vary:

- culprit
- motive
- incidental clues
- dialogue
- minor characters
- exact locations
- red herrings
- social relationships

The AI must preserve:

- one coherent solution
- fair clue placement
- consistent evidence
- fictional principal characters
- historically plausible atmosphere
- the player's freedom to investigate

# Design Notes

This adventure demonstrates:

- investigation
- NPC interviews
- fair clue placement
- varied culprit selection
- historical atmosphere with fictional events
- protecting hidden motives
- restrained use of suggested actions
